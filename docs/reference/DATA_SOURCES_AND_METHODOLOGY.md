## 4. Mathematical Modeling & Statistical Formulations

### A. Population & Working-Age Projections
For district $d$ at elapsed month $t \in [0, 191]$ from baseline January 2011 ($t=0$):
$$\text{Pop}_d(t) = \text{Pop}_{d, 2011} \times \left(1 + r_d\right)^{\frac{t}{12}}$$
$$\text{WorkingAgePop}_d(t) = \text{Pop}_d(t) \times 0.665$$
$$\text{LabourForce}_d(t) = \text{WorkingAgePop}_d(t) \times \left(0.505 + 0.002 \cdot \frac{t}{12} + 0.008 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi t}{12}\right)\right)$$
Where $r_d$ is the annualized intercensal growth rate (varying from $-0.002$ in depopulating agrarian coastal districts to $+0.024$ in rapid urbanization industrial corridors like Chengalpattu and Tiruvallur).

### B. Sectoral Employment & Economic Density
$$\text{Employed}_{s, d}(t) = \text{LabourForce}_d(t) \times \theta_{s, \text{tier}} \times \left(1 + g_s\right)^{\frac{t}{12}} \times \Phi_{\text{season}}(s, t) \times \Omega_{\text{shock}}(s, t) \times \epsilon_t$$
Where:
* $\theta_{s, \text{tier}}$ represents the economic density multiplier (e.g. Pune/Chennai IT multiplier = $6.2\times - 6.8\times$ vs. Gadchiroli/Nandurbar = $0.2\times - 0.4\times$, enforcing the required 4x to 8x industrial disparity).
* $g_s$ is the structural growth coefficient (IT: 8.6%, Healthcare: 6.8%, Manufacturing: 4.6%, Construction: 4.2%, Textiles: 3.2%).
* $\Phi_{\text{season}}(s, t)$ captures monsoon contraction ($-22\%$ in Construction) and festive expansion ($+15\%$ in Textiles).
* $\epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(1.0, 0.015)$ introduces calibrated Gaussian microdata noise.

### C. COVID-19 Pandemic Dynamics & Non-Linear Recovery
During the pandemic window (2020 Q1 to 2021 Q1):
$$\Omega_{\text{shock}}(s, t) = 1.0 + \delta_s \cdot I_{\text{covid}}(t)$$
Where:
* $\delta_{\text{Construction}} = -0.52$ (52% decline during strict lockdown)
* $\delta_{\text{Manufacturing}} = -0.38$ (38% industrial factory halt)
* $\delta_{\text{Textiles}} = -0.42$ (42% retail and export shock)
* $\delta_{\text{Healthcare}} = +0.44$ (44% acute surge in clinical workforce demand)
* $\delta_{\text{IT-ITeS}} = +0.12$ (12% surge driven by global digital transition)

For post-2021 recovery ($t > 120$):
$$\text{RecoveryMult}(s, t) = \beta_s \cdot \ln(1 + \tau_{\text{post-2021}})$$
Modeling diminishing marginal recovery back to long-term trend lines.

### D. Compounded Wage Inflation Formula
$$\text{AvgWage}_{s, d}(t) = \text{BaseWage}_{s, 2011} \times (1 + 0.055)^{\frac{t}{12}} \times \mu_{\text{metro}} \times \epsilon_{\text{wage}}$$
Where the nominal wage compounds at exactly 5.5% annual statutory inflation, supplemented by a metro cost-of-living multiplier ($\mu_{\text{metro}} = 1.25$ for Mumbai/Delhi/Chennai).

### E. Demographic Proportions via Dirichlet Distributions
To enforce exact integer sum consistency ($\sum \text{Counts} = \text{Total Employed}$) while generating realistic variance:
$$\vec{p}_{\text{age}} \sim \text{Dirichlet}\left(\vec{\alpha}_{\text{age}} \times 120\right)$$
$$\vec{p}_{\text{education}} \sim \text{Dirichlet}\left(\vec{\alpha}_{\text{education}} \times 100\right)$$
Counts are computed via integer floor projection with the residual added to the modal cohort, ensuring zero round-off discrepancies.

## 5. Master Schema & Feature Specifications

Both `lmis_district_monthly_2011_2026.csv` and `lmis_district_quarterly_2011_2026.csv` share a unified schema:

```text
1. State_Name                                (String: Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Delhi, West Bengal)
2. State_LGD_Code                            (String / Integer: 27, 33, 07, 19)
3. District_Name                             (String: Official district name)
4. District_LGD_Code                         (String / Integer: LGD standardized code)
5. District_Total_Population_Est             (Integer: Extrapolated population)
6. Timestamp                                 (String: YYYY-MM for monthly, YYYY-Qx for quarterly)
7. Timestamp_Month / Timestamp_Quarter       (String: Temporal identifiers)
8. Year / Month / Quarter                    (Integer: Calendar markers)
9. Sector_Name                               (String: 5 target sectors)
10. NIC_Code                                 (String: Division 10-32, 41-43, 62-63, 86, 13-14)
11. NCO_Code                                 (Integer: 8110, 7111, 2511, 2211, 8261)
12. NSQF_Level                               (String: 3-6, 2-5, 5-8, 4-8, 2-5)
13-15. Gender Counts                         (Male_Employed_Count, Female_Employed_Count, Transgender_Employed_Count)
16-18. Gender Shares                         (Male_Share_Pct, Female_Share_Pct, Transgender_Share_Pct)
19-21. Age Group Counts                      (Age_15_24_Count, Age_25_54_Count, Age_55_Plus_Count)
22-24. Age Group Shares                      (Age_15_24_Pct, Age_25_54_Pct, Age_55_Plus_Pct)
25-28. Education Qualification Counts        (Edu_Below_Primary, Edu_Sec_HrSec, Vocational_ITI, Graduate_Above)
29-32. Education Qualification Shares        (Edu_Below_Primary_Pct, Edu_Sec_HrSec_Pct, Vocational_ITI_Pct, Graduate_Above_Pct)
33. Active_Job_Vacancies                     (Integer: Employer demand signal)
34. Total_Employed_Workers                   (Integer: Current employed stock)
35. eShram_Registered_Seekers                (Integer: Unorganised supply signal)
36. Avg_Monthly_Wage_INR                     (Integer: Monthly nominal wage earnings)
37. Unemployment_Rate_Pct                    (Float: Constrained between 3.5% and 12.0%)
```
