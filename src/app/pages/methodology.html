Methodology

How the Demand Index is built, how the doubt on each forecast is measured, and what would change with real data.

This page holds only what the other pages do not. The forecasting engine, the six readings and the test results are on About, the data and where it came from are on Resources, and every number is served by the read-only API.

Each step below can be checked against the dashboard. The full write-up, with the code behind every step, is the methodology document in the project's repository.

The Demand Index, step by step

Several imperfect signals about hiring become one number for each district and trade. Only the signals that count openings are used.

SignalAvailable fromIn the index?
Job vacanciesJanuary 2000Yes. The reference signal, and the only one in forecast months.
Job-portal postingsJanuary 2000Yes, in history. A share of all openings, and a growing one as hiring moves online.
National Career Service vacanciesJuly 2015Yes, from its launch. Before it starts, it adds nothing.
Quarterly hiring surveyJanuary 2009No. An index relative to its own series cannot be compared between districts. It is a model input.
Labour-force survey estimateJuly 2017No. It measures employment, not demand. It is a model input.
Registered job seekersJanuary 2000No. It is supply, not demand. It is forecast as a target.
1

Openings per worker

For each signal, the logarithm of the signal plus 1, divided by employed workers. Dividing removes the size of the district, the logarithm turns ratios into differences, and the plus 1 keeps empty cells defined.

2

Position among peers

That figure is standardised against its peers, the districts of that trade in the same month, to an average of 0 and a spread of 1. This removes any national shift, such as the portals' growing coverage or the launch of the National Career Service.

3

Composite

The average of the signals available, rescaled to a spread of 1 across the districts of the trade. A signal that has not started contributes nothing.

4

National level

Steps 2 and 3 are relative, so a trade whose demand rose everywhere would not move. A level term carries the trade's national openings per worker, measured from its 2018 to 2019 average in units of the district spread, so the index rises and falls over the years.

5

The index

100 times Φ of (composite plus level), where Φ is the normal distribution function. So 50 is the median district, 84 is one spread above it and 16 one below. It cannot leave 0 to 100.

6

Forecast months and totals

Forecast months use forecast vacancies and employed workers only, because the other signals measure the same openings and are not forecast. States, sectors, trades and India are averages of their series, weighted by employed workers.

What was checked

Forecast months track the full index

In every year from 2012 to 2025, the version that uses vacancies alone, as forecast months must, agrees with the three-signal index at 0.89 to 0.94 (0.93 in 2025), differing by 8 to 10 points on average.

Scale and bounds

Every value lies between 0 and 100, and before the level term every trade-month has an average of 0 and a spread of 1, checked by tests on synthetic data.

Not checked

Agreement with real hiring, because the data is simulated. The signals are weighted equally by choice, not estimated.

How the doubt is measured

The ranges and the confidence labels are described on the About page. This is how they are set.

A range from past errors

For a forecast some months ahead, the 90% range adds the 5th and 95th percentile of earlier errors on the log change, for the same measure at one or two months either side, widened until at least 400 errors from 8 forecast dates are pooled. Only years the model had not seen are used, so the range does not shrink when the fit to past data improves.

A range for each level

Neighbouring series tend to miss in the same direction at once, so the range of a state or of India is not the sum of the ranges of its series. It is set from the errors of that level itself, for district and sector, state and sector, state, sector, trade and India.

A label that must be earned

For each measure, trade and twelve-month band, the average miss (WAPE) of earlier forecasts decides the label: high up to 8%, medium up to 20%, low above, and one level lower for a small series. A label is useful only if later error rises from high to low. It does for employed workers and vacancies, not reliably for job seekers.

Judgements, and what changes with real data

Some choices are conventions rather than results, and they are listed so that they can be challenged. The limits of the numbers are on the About page.

ChoiceWhat it is
ThresholdsThe flag percentiles, the label limits and the severity ratios are conventions, fixed before the test years were opened, not tuned.
Leaving out the shockLeaving the 2020 to 2023 shock out of the final fit, as the About page describes, is a judgement about a simulated shock, not a tuned choice. Its side effect is lumpier job-seeker growth where the horizon passes 24 months.
