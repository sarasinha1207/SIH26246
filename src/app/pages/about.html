About the project

KaushalSetu is an AI-enabled labour market intelligence and skill demand-supply forecasting engine for the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE).

India trains lakhs of candidates every year, but training capacity is often out of step with what employers need in a district and trade. Planners usually find out only after placement results arrive. This project forecasts employment, job vacancies and registered job seekers for every district and trade, five years ahead, with a range and a confidence label on every number, and sets the forecast openings against the training seats on offer, so that seats, camps and attention can go where they are needed.

The figures on this site are simulated to behave like official statistics. They show how the method works, not what is happening in any real district.

Forecasts

Sixty months ahead for 7,925 district and trade series, from January 2026 to December 2030.

Honest ranges

A usual and a wider 90% range, a simple baseline to compare with and a confidence label beside every forecast.

Demand and capacity

A Demand Index from 0 to 100, a capacity gap against training seats, and early warnings for the tightest and loosest tenth of districts.

How it works

From raw counts to published forecasts.

  1. 1From January 2000

    Collect

    Monthly counts of employed workers, vacancies and registered job seekers, for 7,925 district and trade series.

  2. 2Before any use

    Check

    Counts are audited for gaps and errors, and the five trades of a sector are made to add up to the sector. Shock periods such as COVID and the e-Shram launch are marked so they cannot mislead the model.

  3. 32000 to 2009

    Learn

    The model learns recent movement, trends and seasonal patterns, using only what was known at each forecast date. Settings are tuned on 2010 to 2014.

  4. 42015 to 2025

    Test

    It is scored once on 2015 to 2019, then once on the shock years 2020 to 2025, against simple forecasts that need no model.

  5. 5Next 60 months

    Forecast

    Each series is forecast one to sixty months ahead, with two 90% ranges and a confidence label.

  6. 6For every forecast month

    Index, flag and compare

    The Demand Index and the capacity gap are computed, districts are ranked and flagged, and the files are published with checksums.

Model engine overview and core principles

The engine is built to be believed only as far as it has earned. It forecasts three measures for every district and trade, and it is judged against simple baselines on years it has never seen. These principles shape every choice.

Never peek at the future

Every feature uses data available at the forecast date. A test cuts the data at the forecast month, rebuilds the features and checks they are unchanged.

Judge on unseen years

Validation and testing are split by calendar time. The test years 2015 to 2019 and the shock years 2020 to 2025 were each opened once, and nothing was changed afterwards.

Compare with something simple

Three simple forecasts sit beside the model, and the best of them in each case is the comparison, so anyone can see how much the model adds, and where it adds little.

Show the doubt

Every forecast carries a usual and a wider 90% range and a confidence label, high, medium or low, earned in testing.

Judge districts against peers

Flags and the Demand Index compare a district with the other districts of the same trade in the same month.

Say where it is weak

Job-seeker forecasts, vacancies in the fifth year, and anything in a shock year are weaker, and the site says so beside the numbers.

Be reproducible

Fixed random seeds, recorded settings and file checksums mean a rerun gives identical forecasts.

Keep the assumptions visible

Training seats are held at today's level because no seat plans are forecast, and the page where seats are compared with openings says so.

Keep the data honest

The data is simulated, and every page says so. Results show how the method works, not how it would work on real statistics.

Machine learning engine and architecture

Six stages turn the monthly panel into forecasts. Each stage reads only what the stage before it produced.

1

Data

A monthly panel of 7,925 series: employed workers, vacancies and registered job seekers, with wages, unemployment, training seats, demographics and four demand channels.

2

Features

Recent movement, level against the recent average, volatility, vacancy and seeker rates, training and wage ratios and the demand channels, all built only from data up to the forecast month: 82 inputs.

3

Models

XGBoost, a gradient-boosted tree method. One model per measure forecasts the change in the log of the level, with the number of months ahead as an input, for every horizon from 1 to 60, averaged over three random starts.

4

Reconcile

The five trades of a district and sector are made to add up exactly to the sector forecast, so totals at every level agree.

5

Ranges

Every forecast gets two 90% ranges, set from past errors for the same horizon: a usual one from 2010 to 2019 and a wider one that also uses the shock years.

6

Index, flags and files

The Demand Index, the capacity gap, the early-warning flags and the confidence labels are computed for every series, and the files are written with checksums.

PartWhat it is
AlgorithmXGBoost histogram trees. Three measures, three random starts each: nine models in the final fit.
Inputs82 inputs, chosen from 119 candidates by testing on the validation years. Dropping the inputs that identify a single district or sector cut the validation error for employed workers from 10.9% to 4.5%, because they let the trees memorise the period instead of the series.
Training and testingTrained on 2000 to 2009, tuned on 2010 to 2014, tested once on 2015 to 2019 and once on 2020 to 2025. The split is by calendar time, never random.
TuningA TPE search (Optuna), 20 trials per measure, over learning rate, tree size, penalties and sampling, scored on the validation years only. The number of trees for each twelve-month band of horizons is chosen there too.
Error measureWAPE, the total size of the misses divided by the total size of the actual values. Lower is better.
Final fitThe declared settings are refitted on all data to December 2025, with nothing re-tuned. The training pairs that reach into the 2020 to 2023 shock and recovery are left out, so the forecasts do not repeat that jump as if it were a normal month.
Output per run4,75,500 forecast rows (7,925 series, 60 months) with two ranges and a confidence label each, and the Demand Index, capacity gap and flags built on them. Rerunning gives identical files, and every file carries a checksum.

Demand Index, capacity gap and early warnings

Three readings built on the forecasts, each defined in a few lines so that it can be checked.

Demand Index

A number from 0 to 100 for a district and trade in a month. It compares openings per worker, from vacancies, job-portal postings and the National Career Service, with the other districts of the same trade, and adds a national level term so that it moves over the years. 50 is the median district at the 2018 to 2019 level; forecast months use the forecast vacancies only.

Capacity gap

Forecast openings for each year against the training seats sanctioned in 2025 to 2026, held flat, and the trainees expected to be certified, which is the seats times the 2023 to 2025 certification rate. Seats fall short where a district's share of seats is under 0.6 times its share of openings in the trade, and exceed demand above 1.65 times.

Early warnings

In each trade and month, districts are ranked by forecast vacancies per registered seeker. The top tenth is a shortage alert and the bottom tenth an oversupply alert; the next fifteen per cent either side is a watch. The flag is relative: it says which districts stand out, not that openings exceed seekers.

What to keep in mind

Limits stated plainly, so that the numbers are read as they deserve.

The data is simulated

It behaves like official statistics but describes no real district, so no figure is an official statistic.

Totals need the wider range

Errors of neighbouring series move together, so the usual range for a state or for India held only about six times in ten in calm years and almost never in the shock years. Totals show the wider range as well.

Seats are held flat

The capacity gap assumes today's seats for every year to 2030, and reads best as a ranking of where capacity is stretched rather than a count of unfilled places.

Long horizons are looser

Forecasts beyond two years rest on how districts behaved before 2020, and seeker forecasts are the least reliable. Treat the fifth year as a direction, not a figure.

Ranges for some views are missing

For combinations such as one trade across a state, no checked range exists, so only the forecast itself is drawn and the dashboard says so.

Shocks cannot be forecast

A pandemic or a new registration drive would break the pattern the models learned. The wide range allows for some of it, not all.
