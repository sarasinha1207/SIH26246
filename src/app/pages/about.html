About the project

KaushalSetu is an AI-enabled labour market intelligence and skill demand-supply forecasting engine for the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE).

It forecasts employment, job vacancies and registered job seekers for every district and trade, five years ahead, with a range and a confidence label on every number, and sets the forecast openings against the training seats on offer, so that seats, camps and attention can go where they are needed.

How it works

From raw counts to published forecasts.

  1. 1From January 2000

    Collect

    Monthly counts of employed workers, vacancies and registered job seekers, for 7,925 district and trade series.

  2. 2Before any use

    Check

    Counts are audited for gaps and errors, and the five trades of a sector are made to add up to the sector. Shock periods such as COVID and the e-Shram launch are marked so they cannot mislead the model.

  3. 32000 to 2009

    Learn

    The model learns recent movement, trends and seasonal patterns, using only what was known at each forecast date. Settings are tuned on 2010 to 2014.

  4. 42015 to 2025

    Test

    It is scored once on 2015 to 2019, then once on the shock years 2020 to 2025, against simple forecasts that need no model.

  5. 5Next 60 months

    Forecast

    Each series is forecast one to sixty months ahead, with two 90% ranges and a confidence label.

  6. 6For every forecast month

    Index, flag and compare

    The Demand Index and the capacity gap are computed, districts are ranked and flagged, and the files are published with checksums.

Machine learning engine and architecture

One model for each of the three measures, judged against simple forecasts on years it never saw. It is built to be believed only as far as it has earned, and every input uses only what was known at the forecast date.

PartWhat it is
AlgorithmXGBoost histogram trees. Three measures, three random starts each: nine models in the final fit. Each forecasts the change in the log of the level, with the number of months ahead as an input, for every horizon from 1 to 60.
Inputs82 inputs, chosen from 119 candidates by testing on the validation years: recent movement, level against the recent average, volatility, vacancy and seeker rates, training and wage ratios and the demand channels. Dropping the inputs that identify a single district or sector cut the validation error for employed workers from 10.9% to 4.5%, because they let the trees memorise the period instead of the series. A test cuts the data at the forecast month, rebuilds the inputs and checks they are unchanged.
TuningA TPE search (Optuna), 20 trials per measure, over learning rate, tree size, penalties and sampling, scored on the validation years only. The number of trees for each twelve-month band of horizons is chosen there too.
Error measureWAPE, the total size of the misses divided by the total size of the actual values. Lower is better.
Final fitThe declared settings are refitted on all data to December 2025, with nothing re-tuned. The training pairs that reach into the 2020 to 2023 shock and recovery are left out, so the forecasts do not repeat that jump as if it were a normal month.
ReconcileThe five trades of a district and sector are made to add up exactly to the sector forecast, so totals at every level agree.
RangesEvery forecast gets two 90% ranges, set from past errors for the same horizon: a usual one from 2010 to 2019 and a wider one that also uses the shock years. How they are set is on the Methodology page.

Demand Index, capacity gap, severity and early warnings

Six readings built on the forecasts, each defined in a few lines so that it can be checked. The Demand Index is built step by step, with a worked example, on the Methodology page, and every number is served by the read-only API.

Demand Index

A number from 0 to 100 for a district and trade in a month. It compares openings per worker, from vacancies, job-portal postings and the National Career Service, with the other districts of the same trade, and moves over the years with the trade's national level. 50 is the median district at the 2018 to 2019 level.

Capacity gap

Forecast openings for each year against the training seats sanctioned in 2025 to 2026, held flat, and the trainees expected to be certified, which is the seats times the 2023 to 2025 certification rate. Seats fall short where a district's share of seats is under 0.6 times its share of openings in the trade, and exceed demand above 1.65 times.

Early warnings

In each trade and month, districts are ranked by forecast vacancies per registered seeker. The top tenth is a shortage alert and the bottom tenth an oversupply alert; the next fifteen per cent either side is a watch. The flag is relative: it says which districts stand out, not that openings exceed seekers.

Severity ranking

Score = the natural log of the trainees expected to be certified in a year divided by the average monthly openings. Below zero is undersupply and above zero oversupply; a ratio of 2 or more either way is severe, 1.5 high and 1.25 moderate. Trades, states, state and trade, districts, district and sector, and district and trade can be ranked for any year to 2030, within any filter.

Seat scenarios

Multiply the seats by a percentage from a chosen year and see the ratio and the severity before and after, and the seats needed to reach a target. The certification rate and the forecast openings stay as they are, and no delay is modelled between sanctioning a seat and certifying a trainee.

Alerts that begin or end

The actual tightness of December 2025 is compared with the forecast at 12, 36 or 60 months, to list the series that enter or leave an alert tail and the first month they are in it. Alerts mostly persist: about one in eight of today's is out of its tail after 12 months, and about as many move in.

What to keep in mind

Limits stated plainly, so that the numbers are read as they deserve. The judgements behind them are on the Methodology page.

The data is simulated

It behaves like official statistics but describes no real district, so no figure is an official statistic.

Totals need the wider range

Errors of neighbouring series move together, so the usual range for a state or for India held only about six times in ten in calm years and almost never in the shock years. Totals show the wider range as well.

Seats are held flat

The capacity gap assumes today's seats for every year to 2030 unless you test another number with a seat scenario, and reads best as a ranking of where capacity is stretched rather than a count of unfilled places.

Long horizons are looser

Forecasts beyond two years rest on how districts behaved before 2020, and seeker forecasts are the least reliable. Treat the fifth year as a direction, not a figure.

Ranges for some views are missing

For combinations such as one trade across a state, no checked range exists, so only the forecast itself is drawn and the dashboard says so.

Shocks cannot be forecast

A pandemic or a new registration drive would break the pattern the models learned. The wide range allows for some of it, not all.
