About the project

LMIS India is an AI-enabled labour market intelligence and skill demand-supply forecasting engine for the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE).

India trains lakhs of candidates every year, but training capacity is often out of step with what employers need in a district and sector. Planners usually find out only after placement results arrive. This project forecasts employment, job vacancies and registered job seekers for every district and sector, twelve months ahead, with a range and a confidence label on every number, so that seats, camps and centres can be planned before a mismatch appears.

The figures on this site are simulated to behave like official statistics. They show how the method works, not what is happening in any real district.

How it works

One monthly cycle, from raw counts to published forecasts.

  1. 1From January 2011

    Collect

    Monthly counts of employed workers, vacancies and registered job seekers, for 280 district and sector series.

  2. 2Before any use

    Check

    Counts are audited for gaps and errors. Shock periods such as COVID and the e-Shram launch are marked so they cannot mislead the model.

  3. 32011 to early 2022

    Learn

    The model learns recent movement, sector trends and seasonal patterns, using only what was known at each forecast date.

  4. 42023 to 2026

    Test

    It is scored on months it has never seen, against simple forecasts that need no model.

  5. 5Next 12 months

    Forecast

    Each series is forecast one to twelve months ahead, with a 90% range and a confidence label.

  6. 6Every month

    Flag and refresh

    Districts are ranked and flagged, the files are published, and the cycle repeats with the newest month.

Model engine overview and core principles

The engine is built to be believed only as far as it has earned. It forecasts three measures for every district and sector, and it is judged against simple baselines on months it has never seen. These principles shape every choice.

Never peek at the future

Every feature uses data available at the forecast date. A test cuts the data at the forecast month, rebuilds the features and checks they are unchanged.

Judge on unseen months

Validation and testing are split by calendar time. The final test period was opened once, and nothing was changed afterwards.

Compare with something simple

A baseline forecast sits beside every number, so anyone can see how much the model adds, and where it adds little.

Show the doubt

Every forecast carries a 90% range and a confidence label, high, medium or low, earned in testing.

Judge districts against peers

Flags are relative to the same sector in the same month, and each carries an absolute reading of whether openings really exceed seekers.

Say where it is weak

Vacancy forecasts and early-warning flags add little over the baseline, so both are marked low confidence.

Be reproducible

Fixed random seeds, recorded settings and file checksums mean a rerun gives identical forecasts.

Stay current

Each month adds the newest data, refits the declared settings and scores earlier forecasts against what happened.

Keep the data honest

The data is simulated, and every page says so. Results show how the method works, not how it would work on real statistics.

Machine learning engine and architecture

Six stages turn the monthly panel into forecasts. Each stage reads only what the stage before it produced.

1

Data

A monthly panel of 280 series: employed workers, vacancies and registered job seekers, with wages, unemployment, training seats and demographics.

2

Features

Recent movement, level against the recent average, seasonal patterns, vacancy-to-seeker ratios, sector trends and calendar, all built only from data up to the forecast month.

3

Models

XGBoost, a gradient-boosted tree method. One model per measure forecasts the change from a recent level for every horizon from 1 to 12 months, averaged over five random starts.

4

Blend

Where earlier validation showed it helps, the forecast is blended with a simple baseline, last value plus the usual seasonal shift.

5

Ranges

Every forecast gets a 90% range, fixed from validation errors for employed workers and vacancies, and adaptive for job seekers, following recent errors.

6

Flags and files

Districts are ranked within their sector and flagged. The forecast, range, baseline, confidence label and flag files are written for every series.

PartWhat it is
AlgorithmXGBoost histogram trees. Three measures, five random starts each: 15 models per run.
FeaturesEach model uses only the features that earned their place in testing: 36 for employed workers, 29 for vacancies and 68 for job seekers, chosen from 77 candidates.
Training and testingTrained on January 2011 to February 2022, tuned on March to December 2022, tested once on January 2023 to December 2026. The split is by calendar time, never random.
TuningA TPE search (Optuna) over learning rate, tree size, penalties and sampling, scored on rolling-origin folds inside the training years. A new setting replaced the old only if it was clearly better.
Error measureWAPE, the total size of the misses divided by the total size of the actual values. Lower is better.
Monthly refreshThe declared settings are refitted on all history up to the newest month, with nothing re-tuned.
Output per run10,080 forecast rows (280 series, 3 measures, 12 horizons) and 3,360 flag rows. Rerunning gives identical files, and every file carries a checksum.
